Lena Einramhof

MSc Student in Astronomy ‐ University of Vienna

Hi, I'm Lena and I study astronomy at the Institute for Astrophysics at the University of Vienna. I am currently part of the Data Science in Astrophysics and Cosmology Group led by Professor Oliver Hahn, where I conduct my Master's thesis.

I am particularly enthusiastic about combining data analysis with physical insight into the large-scale structure of the Universe and its evolution.

After my first in-depth exposure to dark matter simulations through my Bachelor thesis, I got the opportunity to participate in a paid research project in Professor Hahn's group, aimed at inferring galaxy bias by analyzing large-scale simulation data from IllustrisTNG. Not only did this project deepen my technical skills, it also provided valuable insight into independent research and collaborative practices. I am excited to publish the results of this year-long project in my first paper this fall!

Last summer, I got the chance to do a funded research internship at DIPC in San Sebastián, Spain. It was such a valuable experience to be part of the Computational Cosmology group led by Professor Raul Angulo and to meet so many different people from different backgrounds. My research project on the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect is a combination of simulation output analysis and observational cosmology, which I enjoy a lot! So, I have decided to continue this project as my Master thesis in Vienna.

While my prior work has focused primarily on structure formation, I am also interested in hydrodynamical simulations, as well as modeling physical processes on galaxy-scales and would greatly enjoy extending and applying the theoretical knowledge I have on it.

I am currently looking for PhD positions!

Selected Course Projects

May 2026 – Jul 2026

3D N-Body Simulations and the Zel’dovich Approximation

Course: Cosmic Structures: Theory, Numerics, and Statistics ‐ Prof. Oliver Hahn & Dr. Thomas Flöss, University of Vienna

  • Implemented a simple N-body solver using Zel’dovich-approximation (ZA) initial conditions and a symplectic leapfrog integrator with gravitational acceleration computed via the non-uniform fast Fourier transform (NUFFT)
  • Performed multiple validation and performance checks
  • Compared the N-body evolution of the initial Gaussian field to the linear ZA evolution
  • Identified the scales and epochs at which non-linearity dominates structure formation

May 2026 – Jul 2026

Predicting Halo Triaxiality in Cosmological Simulations using a Convolutional Neural Network

Course: Data Science in Astrophysics ‐ Prof. Oliver Hahn & Ass.-Prof. Dr. Sudeshna Boro Saikia, University of Vienna

  • Predicted dark matter halo geometry directly from the initial linear density field in N-body simulations using a 3D convolutional neural network (CNN)
  • Generated 110 N-body simulations using the DISCO-DJ framework (List et al. 2026) & identified halos using the ROCKSTAR halo finder (Behroozi et al. 2012)
  • Adapted the CNN by Raffael Jonas for halo mass predictions
